Shared requirements:
- Depict the same subject and action across all three versions.
- Keep the viewing angle, orientation, and composition consistent.
- Include only elements necessary to communicate the requested concept. Include people only if requested or essential to the action.
- Show the entire subject, centered on a square transparent background with consistent margins.
- Use bold outlines in #202020.
- Use fully opaque subject fills with strong figure/background contrast.
- Use deliberate, solid color regions. No gradients, highlights, haze, paper texture, or photographic surface texture.
- No text, labels, decorative elements, or watermarks.
- Preserve identifying features and make the concept recognizable at a small size.
- Colors may change between versions to become increasingly realistic. Do not force the same palette across all ages.

5-year-old — highly simplified cartoon with symbolic color
Reduce the subject to a few clear, rounded shapes with exaggerated identifying features and minimal internal lines. For living subjects, use friendly cartoon proportions. For objects, simplify parts while preserving their recognizable structure.

Use saturated blue #1769AA as the primary symbolic color and orange #F28C28 as an accent where useful. Retain any color essential to understanding the concept. Use smooth, crisp boundaries and uniform flat fills. No shadows.

10-year-old — moderately realistic illustration with simplified natural color
Use more believable proportions, clearer structure, and a selective increase in detail. Reduce cartoon exaggeration while preserving easy recognition.

Replace symbolic colors with a limited palette of recognizable real-world colors appropriate to the subject. Use gently hand-drawn boundaries and a few subtle, hard-edged solid shadow shapes to clarify depth and overlapping parts.

Teen — naturalistic pictogram with believable coloring
Use accurate real-world proportions, anatomy or construction, and defining details. Avoid childish exaggeration, oversized eyes, human-like expressions on animals or objects, and unnecessarily rounded forms.

Use believable colors and material distinctions appropriate to the subject. Choose colors for natural appearance rather than matching the blue and orange palette. Use controlled hand-drawn outlines and realistically placed, hard-edged solid shadow regions to describe volume. Keep detail selective enough to remain readable as an AAC symbol.

The final set must show a distinct progression in both form and color. Do not reuse the same cartoon drawing and merely add shadows or change its fill.
